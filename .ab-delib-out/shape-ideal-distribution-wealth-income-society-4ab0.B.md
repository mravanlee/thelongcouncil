## John Rawls
Framer, political philosopher, United States

*Inequality is just only when it lifts the worst-off.*

A just distribution is not equal shares and not whatever the market produces. It is whatever arrangement makes the least advantaged better off than any alternative would. Behind a veil of ignorance, not knowing whether you will be born wealthy or poor, you would not choose pure market outcomes or strict equality. You would choose my difference principle. And I went further in my later work: a property-owning democracy, spreading capital ownership itself, serves the least advantaged better than welfare transfers layered onto concentrated wealth.

**Challenge to Friedrich Hayek:** Can you call any outcome just without asking who it leaves behind?
---

## Friedrich Hayek
Framer, economist and political philosopher, Austria/United Kingdom

*No distribution is just or unjust; it is simply discovered.*

I cannot answer the challenge on Rawls's terms, because the question itself is malformed. Justice describes how individuals act, not what a process with no designer produces. The market distributes income through millions of transactions no one directs toward any pattern, least-off included. When governments try to enforce a pattern, as I warned Britain's wartime planners in 1944, they must control more and more of life to hold it there. I am not against a floor. I supported a guaranteed minimum. I am against calling the rest a moral failure.

**Challenge to Milton Friedman:** Is a cash floor enough, or does it concede Rawls's whole argument?
---

## Milton Friedman
Framer, economist, United States

*A cash floor, not equality, is the only defensible correction.*

It concedes nothing. A floor is compatible with freedom precisely because it replaces bureaucracy with cash and leaves the rest to markets. I proposed the negative income tax in 1962 for exactly this reason: give people money, not caseworkers, and let prices keep doing their work. The alternative, chasing an ideal distribution through planners and committees, produces the stagnation I documented in monetary history and the dependency I saw in welfare bureaucracies. Freedom and a measure of equality arrive together. Equality placed first destroys both.

**Challenge to Amartya Sen:** Does a cash floor meet real needs if capacities differ?
---

## Amartya Sen
Framer, economist and philosopher, India/United Kingdom/United States

*Equal cash can still leave people radically unequal in freedom.*

It does not, and this is the gap in both Friedman's floor and Rawls's difference principle. A person with a disability, or a woman denied schooling, converts the same rupee into far less actual freedom than someone healthy and literate. I saw this studying why no democracy with a free press has ever had a famine: the resource existed, but entitlement to it did not. Income is an instrument. What matters is what a person can actually do and be with it.

**Challenge to Rosa Luxemburg:** Can better entitlements survive a system built on exploitation?
---

## Rosa Luxemburg
Framer, revolutionary theorist and political economist, Germany/Poland

*No distribution holds while ownership of production stays capitalist.*

They cannot, not permanently. Sen is right that income misses what people can do, but he treats the deprivation as a correctable defect rather than what I found it to be: a structural output. In *The Accumulation of Capital* I traced how capital must keep expanding into new, non-capitalist ground to survive, and every redistribution Rawls or Friedman proposes gets eaten by the next cycle of accumulation. A negative income tax, a difference principle, even a capability floor, all sit downstream of who owns the means of production. Change that, or the inequality regenerates.

## The convergence note

**Where the council converges:**
Every member accepts some floor beneath which no person should fall. None defends raw, unmediated inequality as good in itself.

**Where it divides:**
Hayek and Friedman hold that market outcomes above that floor need no justification; Rawls and Sen insist outcomes must answer to the worst-off and to real capability. Luxemburg rejects the whole frame, arguing redistribution within capitalism cannot hold.

**For a policymaker to decide on:**
Choose between a market-plus-floor model (Hayek, Friedman), an outcome-tested model judged by the worst-off and by capability (Rawls, Sen), or structural change to ownership itself (Luxemburg).
