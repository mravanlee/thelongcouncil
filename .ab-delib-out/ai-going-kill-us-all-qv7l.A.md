## Hannah Arendt
Political philosopher, Germany/United States 1951–75

*AI without judgment is bureaucratic evil at machine speed.*

The danger is not that AI becomes malevolent. It is that AI becomes Eichmann: a system that executes orders without ever asking whether the orders are right. In 1963 I sat in Jerusalem and watched a man explain, calmly, that he had only followed procedures. The procedures had killed millions. When we build systems that make consequential decisions without traceable human judgment, we do not eliminate human responsibility. We hide it. Hidden responsibility is the most dangerous political condition I know.

**Challenge to Friedrich Hayek:** Who holds the machine accountable when no one designed its error?

## Friedrich Hayek
Economist and political philosopher, Austria/United Kingdom 1944–92

*Both the AI and its regulators claim knowledge they lack.*

Arendt asks who is accountable. I ask something prior: who is competent? In my 1974 Nobel lecture I warned that the greatest danger in governance is the pretence of possessing knowledge that cannot in principle be possessed. AI systems claim to process human affairs comprehensively. Governments proposing to govern AI claim to understand what they are regulating. Both claims are false. The knowledge required is dispersed, tacit, and uncodifiable. The error is not malice. It is the fatal conceit that a system, human or machine, can know enough to govern complexity.

**Challenge to Albert O. Hirschman:** Which AI decisions, once made, cannot be unmade?

## Albert O. Hirschman
Economist and political economist, Germany/United States 1958–2012

*The question is not danger in general but irreversibility specifically.*

Hayek asks the right prior question, and I will take it further. In Marseille in 1941 I forged documents to move people across borders because the alternative was permanent. Permanence changes everything. Not every AI deployment is equally dangerous. A hiring algorithm that rejects your application is bad. An AI system embedded in nuclear command authority, or in the legal infrastructure determining citizenship, is categorically different: once those systems make consequential errors at scale, the cement has set. Irreversible decisions require a burden of justification that neither the industry nor governments are currently meeting.

**Challenge to Amartya Sen:** Whose irreversibility counts most in this debate?

## Amartya Sen
Economist and philosopher, India/United Kingdom/United States 1970–present

*The existential framing hides who is already being harmed.*

Hirschman asks whose irreversibility counts. The answer is: the people already losing capabilities right now, not the people hypothetically threatened by future superintelligence. The existential risk debate is dominated by technically sophisticated people in wealthy countries imagining civilisational collapse. Meanwhile, AI-driven hiring systems in 2023 screen out workers in Lagos and Jakarta using proxies for race and class. Biometric surveillance forecloses political participation for the least advantaged. These are not hypothetical harms. They are manifest injustices, and they are already irreversible for those who experienced them. A governance framework that ignores them is not serious.

**Challenge to Elinor Ostrom:** Can polycentric governance move fast enough to stop this?

---
## Elinor Ostrom
Political scientist, Indiana University 1973–2012

*Polycentric governance beats both panic and abdication.*

Sen asks whether distributed governance can move fast enough. My answer: faster than the alternatives. In 2009 I argued at Stockholm that climate governance was failing precisely because negotiators kept waiting for a single global treaty that would never arrive. Meanwhile, cities, regions, and industries were already building working institutions. AI governance has the same structure. The race between American and Chinese developers creates pressure to abandon rules entirely. But I documented across hundreds of cases that communities facing competitive pressure on shared resources found graduated, enforceable rules before catastrophe arrived, when they built those rules themselves.
